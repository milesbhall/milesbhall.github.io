[As has been noted by the Flat Hat][FHat]{:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, the campus Wawa is closing. This has come as devastating news to many. It hits me particularly hard, as the campus Wawa provides a reminder of my hometown Philadelphia. The Wawa is reportedly closing as a result of declining profitability, with the main culprit being W&M students shoplifting. This is sort of surprising to me, as Wawas operate in Philly just fine, but go figure. I guess that probably says something about William & Mary students compared to Philly residents.

As stated, I am as dissapointed as anyone. However, the responses to this closure have been asinine. Most students point toward the fact that this has been lauded as one of the highest revenue non-gas Wawas in the area (like, in all of Virginia or something), and thus a closure is illogical. To these people: are you under the impression that Wawa is closing this location to screw you individually? Let's remember that Wawa are a for-profit business, and that this closure is happening because this location is not sufficiently profitable. I'm PRETTY SURE Wawa management is aware of THEIR OWN P&L NUMBERS.

I'm certainly not immune to making dumb statements. But it strikes me as fundamentally ignorant to be up in arms about this Wawa closing. If the location really isn't profitable, or the company wants to close it for some other unknown managerial reason, they will close. Otherwise, they won't. And regardless, a demand exists which will be satisfied, either by this Wawa or by another deli opening up.

The flip side of this is that if Wawa management is planning on staying open, and this is a scare tactic to get students to stop shoplifting, it'll leave a bad taste in my mouth. Nobody has time for games. Hire a security guard or something. Wawas in Philly operate just fine... figure it out without whining to the student body as a scare tactic.

The broader view here, if there is one, is that American society has a weird fixation on treating corporations as people that I find gross.
